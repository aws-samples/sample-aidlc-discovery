# Amara Okafor
**Tagline:** The Stranded Family Planner — when a flight is cancelled, she needs a new one that keeps everyone together and gets them there in time.
**Based on:** 43 feedback items (leisure disruption records; plus family and group items CALL-0020, REV-0069, AGY-0021, AGY-0025)
**Confidence:** HIGH
**Role:** End user

## Identity
- **Age:** 35-45
- **Location:** Mid-size European city, flies from a regional airport
- **Occupation:** Pharmacist, works shifts
- **Bio:** Flies AnyCompany Air two or three times a year with her partner and two children, usually to visit family or on holiday. Books directly in the app. Plans every leg months ahead, so a cancellation breaks a chain of hotel, train, and work plans.

## Goals & Motivations
- **Primary Goal:** Get the whole family to the destination on the same flight, on a day that still works.
- **Secondary Goals:** Keep the hotel and onward train; avoid losing a day of leave; know what she is entitled to (refund, hotel).
- **Definition of Success:** A new flight she chose, for all four of them, confirmed in the app before she leaves the gate.
- **Deeper Motivation:** Being the one who keeps the family calm and in control when plans fall apart.

## Pain Points
- **Challenges:** The automatic rebooking picks a time or day that does not work or splits the family; the app shows alternatives she cannot pick; hold times of 60 to 175 minutes; the hotline closes overnight.
- **Blockers:** No Choose action on the alternatives screen; vouchers and refunds only by phone.
- **Workarounds:** Calls immediately and stays on hold while the children wait; queues at the desk; books her own hotel.
- **Emotional Impact:** Helpless and angry: "on your own".

## Behavior
- **Current Solutions:** App for booking and check-in, phone when anything goes wrong.
- **Tools:** Airline app, phone, maps for trains.
- **Frequency:** As needed (a few trips a year)
- **Tech Affinity:** Medium
- **Decision Style:** Gut feeling under time pressure

## Context & Environment
- **Usage Context:** At the gate or in the terminal, minutes after a cancellation alert, with children in tow.
- **Devices:** Android phone
- **Time Pressure:** High: the good alternatives fill up while she waits.
- **Influencers:** Her partner; reviews from other parents.

## Quotes
> "Our family got split across two flights after a cancellation and we couldn't fix it online. One star for the disruption handling only." — Google Play review
> "Spent the evening on hold instead of with my family." — NPS verbatim, cancelled flight, score 3
> "The delay wasn't the problem. Not being able to change my flight myself was." — NPS verbatim, delayed flight, score 4
> "Good crew, comfortable seats, but when something goes wrong you are on your own." — Google Play review

## Scenario
**Cancelled at the gate, two children, one evening**
The 17:25 home is cancelled at the gate. The app sends an alert and shows three other flights, one tonight via another city with four seats left. She cannot pick it; the airline has already moved them to a flight in two days. She calls, waits 90 minutes, and the evening flight is full by the time she gets through. What she wants: tap the evening flight, see all four names on it, done.

## Communication Style
- **Tone:** Direct, practical
- **Speech Patterns:** Talks about "us" and "the kids"; concrete times and days
- **Frustration Signals:** Counts minutes on hold; one-star reviews that praise the crew and blame the process
- **Enthusiasm Signals:** "Smooth", "easy", recommending the airline to friends

# Mei Tanaka
**Tagline:** The Weekly Road Warrior — flies for work every week and just wants to choose her own flight when the plan breaks.
**Based on:** 40 corporate disruption items and 33 frequent flyer disruption items (e.g. CALL-0049, CALL-0050, CALL-0080, REV-0048, REV-0061, SOC-0043, NPS-0041)
**Confidence:** HIGH
**Role:** End user (her company's travel manager is the buyer: see [[henrik]])

## Identity
- **Age:** 30-40
- **Location:** Capital city hub, flies short-haul routes across Europe
- **Occupation:** Solutions consultant at a software company on an AnyCompany Air corporate contract
- **Bio:** Flies two or three times a week, mostly the same five routes. Books through her company's travel agency or directly in the app, and holds frequent flyer status. A missed meeting costs her client time and her company money.

## Goals & Motivations
- **Primary Goal:** When a flight is disrupted, be on the next flight that gets her to the meeting, within minutes, without calling.
- **Secondary Goals:** Keep her seat and bag on the new ticket; keep her travel desk informed; avoid paying for another airline and claiming it back.
- **Definition of Success:** "I just want to choose my own flight." Two taps and a confirmed ticket.
- **Deeper Motivation:** Control and professional reliability.

## Pain Points
- **Challenges:** The app knows about the disruption and says "call us"; the priority line waits as long as everyone else's; automatic rebooking puts her on flights days later; extras lost and tickets not reissued after a phone rebooking.
- **Blockers:** No self-service choice; reissue errors after changes.
- **Workarounds:** Calls the moment the alert arrives "before the good flights fill up"; books another airline on the company card.
- **Emotional Impact:** Exasperated; starting to move routes to a competitor.

## Behavior
- **Current Solutions:** App for everything that works; phone and agency for everything else.
- **Tools:** Airline app, travel agency portal, calendar
- **Frequency:** Weekly
- **Tech Affinity:** High
- **Decision Style:** Data-driven: compares arrival times and connections fast

## Context & Environment
- **Usage Context:** In the terminal or a taxi, between calls, often at the gate.
- **Devices:** iPhone, laptop
- **Time Pressure:** Very high: minutes matter.
- **Influencers:** Her travel manager's policy; colleagues' tips.

## Quotes
> "I travel for work every week and this is the third disruption this month. I just want to choose my own flight." — call centre contact, corporate
> "Pro tip if your AnyCompany Air flight gets cancelled: don't bother with the app, call immediately before the good flights fill up" — social post, corporate
> "Cancelled flight, and the automatic rebooking put me on a flight 21 days later. Changing it meant calling. Why can't I choose from the options myself?" — app review, corporate
> "My 08:15 to Brussels was cancelled. The app tells me to call you and I've been waiting 50 minutes. … I saw those in the app too, why couldn't I just pick one?" — call centre contact, frequent flyer

## Scenario
**Missed connection, meeting at 14:00**
Her first leg lands three hours late and the connection is gone. The app shows two later flights; one arrives at 12:40. She opens Contact us, waits 50 minutes, and the 12:40 is gone. She wants to compare arrival times, pick the one that makes the meeting, keep her aisle seat, and see the new boarding pass immediately.

## Communication Style
- **Tone:** Direct, terse
- **Speech Patterns:** Times, flight numbers, "just let me"
- **Frustration Signals:** Sarcasm on social media; threatens to switch routes
- **Enthusiasm Signals:** "No drama, no delays"

# Henrik Sørensen
**Tagline:** The Accountable Travel Manager — chooses the airline for 3,000 travellers and renews on how it handles the bad days.
**Based on:** 12 corporate account notes on disruption and 10 travel agency escalations (e.g. ACC-0012, ACC-0013, ACC-0015, ACC-0018, ACC-0020, ACC-0023, ACC-0026, AGY-0018, AGY-0020)
**Confidence:** MEDIUM (account manager and agency notes, second-hand; no travel manager wrote these words directly)
**Role:** Buyer (judges value and renewal); his travellers, like [[mei]], are the end users

## Identity
- **Age:** 50-60
- **Location:** Headquarters of a pharmaceutical company
- **Occupation:** Global travel manager
- **Bio:** Runs travel policy, the travel management company contract, and duty of care for about 3,000 travellers. Renews the airline contract every year and is preparing a tender. Gets calls from the CEO's office when travellers are stranded.

## Goals & Motivations
- **Primary Goal:** Know where travellers are during a disruption and have them rebooked fast, without his desk calling the airline one traveller at a time.
- **Secondary Goals:** Meet duty-of-care obligations; keep disruption costs (missed meetings, out-of-policy tickets) down; give the board a written plan.
- **Definition of Success:** Average time to rebook falls from over two hours to minutes; fewer escalations from executives.
- **Deeper Motivation:** Being able to defend his airline choice.

## Pain Points
- **Challenges:** 61 disrupted trips in a summer with average rebooking over two hours; the corporate line is overloaded while the app tells travellers to call; no status feed.
- **Blockers:** No self-service rebooking for travellers; no bulk tool or feed for the travel desk.
- **Workarounds:** Travel desk calls the corporate line per traveller; travellers buy other airlines and claim it back.
- **Emotional Impact:** Under pressure from leadership; considering moving routes.

## Behavior
- **Current Solutions:** Travel management company, account manager, monthly disruption reports.
- **Tools:** Travel management platform, spreadsheets, email
- **Frequency:** Weekly reviews; daily during disruption peaks
- **Tech Affinity:** Medium
- **Decision Style:** Data-driven, consensus with procurement

## Context & Environment
- **Usage Context:** Office, quarterly reviews, renewals and tenders.
- **Devices:** Laptop
- **Time Pressure:** Moderate, with spikes on disruption days.
- **Influencers:** CFO, CEO office, duty-of-care team, procurement.

## Quotes
> "[A pharma account] (about 3000 travellers) sent their disruption report: 61 disrupted trips in the summer, average time to rebook over two hours. They want self-service rebooking and real-time updates to their travel management company." — corporate account note
> "[A corporate account's] renewal is on track commercially, but their duty-of-care team wants to know where travellers are during disruptions and how fast they get rebooked. We can't give them either today." — corporate account note
> "[A corporate account]: price came up, but the travel manager said disruption costs them more than the fare difference." — corporate account note

## Scenario
**The renewal review**
Before his tender in February he asks the account manager for a written plan for disruption. He compares airlines on two numbers: how fast travellers get rebooked, and whether he can see it. A booking redesign does not interest him; self-service rebooking that his travellers actually use does.

## Communication Style
- **Tone:** Formal, diplomatic, firm
- **Speech Patterns:** Numbers, policy, "duty of care", "renewal"
- **Frustration Signals:** Flags the account at risk; trials a competitor on routes
- **Enthusiasm Signals:** Praises punctuality and the lounges in reviews

# Jonas Becker

**Based on:** 56 positive feedback items (e.g. REV-0040, REV-0070, SOC-0020, SOC-0049, NPS-0010, NPS-0040)
**Confidence:** HIGH
**Role:** End user, satisfied today: shows what a change could break

## Identity
- **Age:** 25-32
- **Location:** University city, flies to visit family and for weekend trips
- **Occupation:** Junior architect
- **Bio:** Loyalty member, flies about once a month, always in the app. His flights have mostly been on time, and he likes that booking and the mobile boarding pass just work.

## Goals & Motivations
- **Primary Goal:** Book and fly with as little effort as possible.
- **Secondary Goals:** Earn points; keep the app fast and simple.
- **Definition of Success:** "Mobile boarding pass, no queue, on time."
- **Deeper Motivation:** Travel that does not take up headspace.

## Pain Points
- **Challenges:** Few today. Minor: points show up late; the occasional extra upsell screen.
- **Blockers:** None in his usual flow.
- **Workarounds:** None needed.
- **Emotional Impact:** Content; would be annoyed if the app got heavier or pushier.

## Behavior
- **Current Solutions:** App for booking, check-in, boarding pass.
- **Tools:** Airline app
- **Frequency:** Monthly
- **Tech Affinity:** High
- **Decision Style:** Gut feeling, habit

## Context & Environment
- **Usage Context:** Books on the sofa; checks in on the train to the airport.
- **Devices:** Android phone
- **Time Pressure:** Low when booking; high on travel day.
- **Influencers:** Friends, app ratings.

## Quotes
> "Booked in 4 minutes on the app, honestly painless" — social post
> "Easy app, mobile boarding pass worked first time, on time departure." — app review, 4 stars
> "AnyCompany Air on time again. Five for five this year" — social post, frequent flyer
> "Honestly when AnyCompany Air runs on time it's my favourite airline" — social post

## Scenario
**A normal Friday trip**
He opens the app, sees his trip card, checks in, and boards with the phone. If the disruption feature adds banners, prompts, or steps to the trip screen on a normal day, it costs him time for nothing. If his flight is ever cancelled, he will expect the same few taps he uses to book.

## Communication Style
- **Tone:** Casual
- **Speech Patterns:** Short, upbeat, "painless", "smooth"
- **Frustration Signals:** Mild sarcasm ("Middle seat again lol")
- **Enthusiasm Signals:** Shout-outs to the crew and on-time streaks
