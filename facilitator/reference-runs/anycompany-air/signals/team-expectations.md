# Team expectations: AnyCompany Air digital (website and app)
Written 2026-10-30, before the signal analysis. Taken from what each person in the room said at the kickoff (README), in their words, turned into lines the data could prove wrong. Not edited after the analysis.

1. **Head of digital:** We expect website and app bookers to be put off by a dated booking flow, because "our website looks dated next to the competition, and booking is where we make money."
2. **Head of digital (use case owner):** We expect disrupted passengers to be unable to choose a new flight themselves and to have to call or queue, because the call centre queues every summer.
3. **UX designer:** We expect seat buyers to hesitate on upgrades because the seat map does not show what the seat is like, because a competitor's app has a 3D cabin view and some people ask for it on social media.
4. **Customer service operations lead:** We expect summer call queues to be a staffing problem that more agents would solve, because the queues peak when volume peaks.
5. **Head of corporate accounts:** We expect corporate travellers and their travel managers to have problems the leisure-focused feedback hides, because travel managers raise them every week.
6. **Commercial director:** We expect customers to complain mainly about price, because that is what the director hears most.
7. **Engineering lead:** We expect anything that changes a booking after it is made to be the riskiest part of the systems, because the reservations connection is where things break.
8. **Engineering lead (use case owner):** We expect passengers with delayed or lost bags to call because they cannot see where their bag is, because of lost-baggage calls.
9. **Head of digital (use case owner):** We expect frequent flyers to call because points and status progress are confusing or missing, because of loyalty calls.

## What would surprise us
- That booking, the part of the app the team is proudest of, is not where customers hurt.
- That price is not the top complaint.
- That the summer queues are caused by something the digital product does or fails to do, not only by volume.
