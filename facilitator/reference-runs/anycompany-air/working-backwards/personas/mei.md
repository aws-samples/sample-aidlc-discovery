# Mei Tanaka
**Tagline:** The Weekly Road Warrior — flies for work every week and just wants to choose her own flight when the plan breaks.
**Based on:** 40 corporate disruption items and 33 frequent flyer disruption items (e.g. CALL-0049, CALL-0050, CALL-0080, REV-0048, REV-0061, SOC-0043, NPS-0041)
**Confidence:** HIGH
**Role:** End user (her company's travel manager is the buyer: see [[henrik]])

## Identity
- **Age:** 30-40
- **Location:** Capital city hub, flies short-haul routes across Europe
- **Occupation:** Solutions consultant at a software company on an AnyCompany Air corporate contract
- **Bio:** Flies two or three times a week, mostly the same five routes. Books through her company's travel agency or directly in the app, and holds frequent flyer status. A missed meeting costs her client time and her company money.

## Goals & Motivations
- **Primary Goal:** When a flight is disrupted, be on the next flight that gets her to the meeting, within minutes, without calling.
- **Secondary Goals:** Keep her seat and bag on the new ticket; keep her travel desk informed; avoid paying for another airline and claiming it back.
- **Definition of Success:** "I just want to choose my own flight." Two taps and a confirmed ticket.
- **Deeper Motivation:** Control and professional reliability.

## Pain Points
- **Challenges:** The app knows about the disruption and says "call us"; the priority line waits as long as everyone else's; automatic rebooking puts her on flights days later; extras lost and tickets not reissued after a phone rebooking.
- **Blockers:** No self-service choice; reissue errors after changes.
- **Workarounds:** Calls the moment the alert arrives "before the good flights fill up"; books another airline on the company card.
- **Emotional Impact:** Exasperated; starting to move routes to a competitor.

## Behavior
- **Current Solutions:** App for everything that works; phone and agency for everything else.
- **Tools:** Airline app, travel agency portal, calendar
- **Frequency:** Weekly
- **Tech Affinity:** High
- **Decision Style:** Data-driven: compares arrival times and connections fast

## Context & Environment
- **Usage Context:** In the terminal or a taxi, between calls, often at the gate.
- **Devices:** iPhone, laptop
- **Time Pressure:** Very high: minutes matter.
- **Influencers:** Her travel manager's policy; colleagues' tips.

## Quotes
> "I travel for work every week and this is the third disruption this month. I just want to choose my own flight." — call centre contact, corporate
> "Pro tip if your AnyCompany Air flight gets cancelled: don't bother with the app, call immediately before the good flights fill up" — social post, corporate
> "Cancelled flight, and the automatic rebooking put me on a flight 21 days later. Changing it meant calling. Why can't I choose from the options myself?" — app review, corporate
> "My 08:15 to Brussels was cancelled. The app tells me to call you and I've been waiting 50 minutes. … I saw those in the app too, why couldn't I just pick one?" — call centre contact, frequent flyer

## Scenario
**Missed connection, meeting at 14:00**
Her first leg lands three hours late and the connection is gone. The app shows two later flights; one arrives at 12:40. She opens Contact us, waits 50 minutes, and the 12:40 is gone. She wants to compare arrival times, pick the one that makes the meeting, keep her aisle seat, and see the new boarding pass immediately.

## Communication Style
- **Tone:** Direct, terse
- **Speech Patterns:** Times, flight numbers, "just let me"
- **Frustration Signals:** Sarcasm on social media; threatens to switch routes
- **Enthusiasm Signals:** "No drama, no delays"
