# Ingrid Halvorsen
**Tagline:** The Weekly Road Warrior: travels for work every week and loses half a day each time a flight is disrupted, because she cannot pick the new flight she can already see.
**Confidence:** HIGH
**Based on:** 27 feedback items (corporate travellers' own disruption records: 11 calls, 7 social posts, 5 reviews, 4 survey comments)

## Identity
- **Age:** 40-50
- **Location:** Hamburg, Germany
- **Occupation:** Regional sales director at a software company on an AnyCompany Air corporate contract
- **Bio:** Ingrid flies two to four short-haul legs a week to meet customers. She books through her company's travel management company, checks in on the app, and rarely thinks about the airline until something goes wrong. Her company's renewal is coming up, and her travel manager asks her about every bad trip.

## Goals & Motivations
- **Primary Goal:** Get to the meeting, or home, on the best available flight with as little lost time as possible.
- **Secondary Goals:** Keep control of her own schedule; avoid explaining a missed meeting to a customer or her manager.
- **Definition of Success:** A disruption costs her a few minutes on her phone, not an afternoon on hold.
- **Deeper Motivation:** Being reliable to her customers and her team; not feeling helpless at a gate.

## Pain Points
- **Challenges:** Sees alternative flights in the app or on the website but cannot move herself onto one [Known]; automatic rebooking puts her on flights days or weeks later [Known]; the app says "please contact us" while the line has a 60+ minute wait [Known].
- **Blockers:** No action to choose a new flight; the corporate line is overloaded at the same moment as everyone else's.
- **Workarounds:** Calls immediately, before the good flights fill up; queues at the transfer desk; sometimes books another airline and claims it back [Known, from account notes].
- **Emotional Impact:** Frustration turning into distrust: "Everything else is fine until it isn't."

## Behavior
- **Current Solutions:** Phone, airport desk, or the travel management company.
- **Tools:** AnyCompany Air app (check-in, boarding pass), the corporate booking tool, phone.
- **Frequency:** Weekly
- **Tech Affinity:** High
- **Decision Style:** Data-driven and fast: she takes the first option that gets her there today.

## Context & Environment
- **Usage Context:** At the gate, in a taxi, or at a connecting airport, usually minutes after a cancellation or delay notification.
- **Devices:** Smartphone first; laptop in the evening.
- **Time Pressure:** Very high: a meeting or a connection is at stake.
- **Influencers:** Her company's travel manager and duty-of-care policy; colleagues who fly the same routes.

## Quotes
> "Delays happen, I get it. What I don't get is why I can see alternative flights on the website but can't move myself onto one." (corporate traveller, app or web review)
> "I travel for work every week and this is the third disruption this month. I just want to choose my own flight." (corporate traveller, call centre)
> "Cancelled flight, and the automatic rebooking put me on a flight 21 days later. Changing it meant calling. Why can't I choose from the options myself?" (corporate traveller, review)
> "Pro tip if your AnyCompany Air flight gets cancelled: don't bother with the app, call immediately before the good flights fill up" (corporate traveller, social)
> "The disruption experience is the whole product. Everything else is fine until it isn't." (corporate traveller, social)

## Scenario
**Cancelled at the gate, meeting at 9 tomorrow**
Ingrid's evening flight to Milan is cancelled at the gate. The app shows two flights tomorrow morning, but the only button says "contact us", and the corporate line has a 60-minute wait. She calls and waits, watching the 07:20 fill up, while texting her customer that she may be late. She wants to tap the 07:20, get a confirmation, and go to the hotel.

## Communication Style
- **Tone:** Direct
- **Speech Patterns:** Short, practical sentences; times and flight numbers; "I just want to..."
- **Frustration Signals:** Sarcasm on social media; mentions switching routes to a competitor.
- **Enthusiasm Signals:** Quiet praise for things that just work: "on time", "worked first time".

# Daniel Okafor
**Tagline:** The Holiday Family Stuck at Night: a leisure traveller with a partner and two children, cancelled late in the evening, who would fix it himself if the app let him.
**Confidence:** HIGH
**Based on:** 45 feedback items (leisure travellers' disruption records), plus 11 records about groups and families split by rebooking

## Identity
- **Age:** 35-45
- **Location:** Dublin, Ireland
- **Occupation:** Secondary school teacher
- **Bio:** Daniel flies two or three times a year, mostly on family holidays planned months ahead and bought direct on the website. He chose AnyCompany Air over a low-cost carrier because it is more reliable. Last summer a cancelled return flight cost the family a night and a day of school.

## Goals & Motivations
- **Primary Goal:** Get the whole family home together, on a flight that works, without losing a day.
- **Secondary Goals:** Keep the children calm; know where they will sleep tonight; avoid extra costs.
- **Definition of Success:** Within minutes of the cancellation text, the family has a new flight together and knows about the hotel.
- **Deeper Motivation:** Being in control and looking after his family when things go wrong.

## Pain Points
- **Challenges:** Cancellation text at 23:00, phone line opens at 07:00, and by then the good flights are full [Known]; rebooking splits the family across two flights [Known]; hotel and meal vouchers only come through an agent [Known].
- **Blockers:** No self-service action after a cancellation; out-of-hours help is closed.
- **Workarounds:** Spends the evening on hold; queues at the desk; sorts out his own hotel [Known].
- **Emotional Impact:** Helpless and let down: "The airline didn't care once the flight was cancelled."

## Behavior
- **Current Solutions:** Phone and airport desk.
- **Tools:** AnyCompany Air website (booking), app (check-in, boarding passes), phone.
- **Frequency:** As needed (a few trips a year)
- **Tech Affinity:** Medium
- **Decision Style:** Gut feeling under pressure; research-heavy when booking.

## Context & Environment
- **Usage Context:** Hotel room, airport, or holiday rental, often in the evening, with tired children.
- **Devices:** Smartphone.
- **Time Pressure:** High: alternatives fill up while he waits.
- **Influencers:** His partner; reviews; friends' experiences of airlines.

## Quotes
> "They cancelled my return from Paris by text at 23:00 and the phone line opened at 07:00. By then the next flights were full." (leisure traveller, review)
> "Spent the evening on hold instead of with my family." (leisure traveller, survey)
> "Our family got split across two flights after a cancellation and we couldn't fix it online." (frequent flyer, review)
> "The delay wasn't the problem. Not being able to change my flight myself was." (leisure traveller, survey)
> "I'd have done this myself if the app let me." (leisure traveller, call centre)

## Scenario
**Cancelled on the last night of the holiday**
At 23:00 Daniel gets a text: the family's return flight is cancelled and "we will contact you". The app shows the booking as cancelled with no options. The phone line is closed until 7:00. He wants to choose a flight where the four of them sit together, accept a hotel for the night, and put the children to bed knowing how they will get home.

## Communication Style
- **Tone:** Casual, increasingly emotional
- **Speech Patterns:** Family-first framing ("we", "the kids"); times and waiting minutes.
- **Frustration Signals:** Public posts tagging the airline; one-star reviews "for the disruption handling only".
- **Enthusiasm Signals:** Thanks crew by name for helping with the children.

# Helena Kowalska

**Confidence:** LOW
**Based on:** 3 feedback items, all second-hand (a relative's social post, a relative's review, a travel agency escalation), plus 980 disrupted passengers who rebooked at airport desks (survey score −62, no comments). Added at the team's request (team challenge 3); how many passengers are like her is unknown. [Needs evidence]

## Identity
- **Age:** 70-80
- **Location:** Warsaw, Poland
- **Occupation:** Retired pharmacist
- **Bio:** Helena flies twice a year to visit her daughter in Nice, usually with her husband. Her daughter or a travel agency books for them. She has a smartphone for calls and photos, but has never installed the airline's app.

## Goals & Motivations
- **Primary Goal:** Get home safely after a disruption without having to work out an app.
- **Secondary Goals:** Speak to a person; let her daughter help from a distance.
- **Definition of Success:** Someone tells her clearly what her new flight is, or her daughter can fix it for her.
- **Deeper Motivation:** Safety and dignity; not being a burden.

## Pain Points
- **Challenges:** Cannot get through on the phone during a disruption [Known]; booking pages with too many options confuse her [Known].
- **Blockers:** Does not use the app; the phone line is overloaded; her daughter cannot rebook for her online.
- **Workarounds:** Queues at the airport desk; calls her daughter. [Assumed]
- **Emotional Impact:** Anxious and stranded.

## Behavior
- **Current Solutions:** Airport desk, phone, family, travel agency.
- **Tools:** Phone calls, text messages.
- **Frequency:** As needed (twice a year)
- **Tech Affinity:** Low
- **Decision Style:** Consensus: asks her daughter or the agency.

## Context & Environment
- **Usage Context:** At the airport, often after hours of waiting.
- **Devices:** Basic use of a smartphone.
- **Time Pressure:** Moderate; physical tiredness matters more.
- **Influencers:** Her daughter; the travel agent.

## Quotes
All second-hand, from people speaking about passengers like her:
> "My parents are stuck in Nice after a cancellation and can't get through on the phone. They don't know how to use the app, and the app can't help anyway" (frequent flyer, about their parents, social)
> "Booking for my elderly mother was confusing, too many options on one page." (leisure traveller, review)
> "a cleaner booking page for their less technical customers who book directly" (travel agency escalation)

## Scenario
**Stuck in Nice**
Helena and her husband's flight home is cancelled. The desk queue is long and the phone line rings out. She calls her daughter, who opens the airline's website from home and finds the online tool only says to call. Helena needs someone, a person or her daughter, to be able to put her on a new flight and tell her where to go.

## Communication Style
- **Tone:** Polite, anxious
- **Speech Patterns:** Asks for a person; relies on family to explain.
- **Frustration Signals:** Expressed through relatives' posts and reviews.
- **Enthusiasm Signals:** Thanks staff who help in person.

# Karim Haddad
**Tagline:** The Travel Manager Who Gets the CEO's Calls: responsible for thousands of travellers, he hears about every disruption after it happens, and his team rebooks people one at a time by phone.
**Confidence:** MEDIUM
**Based on:** 24 feedback items (12 corporate account notes and 10 travel agency escalations about disruption; 2 calls from assistants rebooking a boss). Heard second-hand through account managers and agencies, so capped at Medium (team challenge 2).

## Identity
- **Age:** 45-55
- **Location:** Brussels, Belgium
- **Occupation:** Global travel manager at a pharmaceutical company with about 3,000 travellers
- **Bio:** Karim negotiates the company's airline contracts and owns duty of care for travellers. His travel desk and the travel management company handle bookings. Renewals and tenders come to him, and the CFO's office calls him when an executive is stranded.

## Goals & Motivations
- **Primary Goal:** Know where his travellers are during a disruption and get them rebooked fast, without his team calling the airline one traveller at a time.
- **Secondary Goals:** Report disruption cost and time-to-rebook; keep travellers in policy; justify the contract at renewal.
- **Definition of Success:** During a cancellation, his desk sees every affected traveller and can rebook them in bulk, or the travellers rebook themselves in policy.
- **Deeper Motivation:** Duty of care and credibility with leadership.

## Pain Points
- **Challenges:** No visibility of travellers during disruption [Known]; rebooking only by phone, one traveller at a time [Known]; the airline's app tells travellers to call while the corporate line is overloaded [Known].
- **Blockers:** No bulk rebooking, no disruption feed to the travel management company, no rebooking action in the agency portal.
- **Workarounds:** Travel desk on the phone; travellers book another airline and expense it [Known].
- **Emotional Impact:** Exposed: he answers for problems he cannot fix.

## Behavior
- **Current Solutions:** Travel desk, travel management company, account manager escalations.
- **Tools:** Corporate booking tool, travel management company portal, disruption reports, email.
- **Frequency:** Weekly (daily in summer)
- **Tech Affinity:** Medium
- **Decision Style:** Data-driven: disruption reports, time to rebook, cost.

## Context & Environment
- **Usage Context:** Office hours and on call; quarterly reviews and renewals with the airline.
- **Devices:** Laptop; phone for escalations.
- **Time Pressure:** High during disruption; renewal deadlines.
- **Influencers:** CFO, duty-of-care team, procurement, the travel management company.

## Quotes
All second-hand, from account manager notes and agency escalations:
> "the reason we get calls from the CEO's office" (a travel manager on disruption, account note)
> "their duty-of-care team wants to know where travellers are during disruptions and how fast they get rebooked. We can't give them either today." (account note)
> "Today their agents call our corporate line one traveller at a time." (account note)
> "the travel manager said disruption costs them more than the fare difference" (account note)
> "The portal shows the disruption but has no rebooking action." (travel agency escalation)

## Scenario
**Sixty-one disrupted trips in a summer**
After a summer of cancellations, Karim compiles a disruption report: 61 disrupted trips, average time to rebook over two hours. In the renewal meeting he asks for self-service rebooking for travellers and real-time updates to his travel management company. He wants a disruption to show up on his desk's screen with every affected traveller, and to rebook them in policy in one go.

## Communication Style
- **Tone:** Formal, diplomatic, firm
- **Speech Patterns:** Business terms: duty of care, renewal, tender, report.
- **Frustration Signals:** Flags the account as at risk; asks for a written plan before a tender; trials a competitor on some routes.
- **Enthusiasm Signals:** Praise for punctuality and network in reviews.

# Sofia Lindqvist
**Tagline:** The Happy Regular: books in minutes, checks in on the app, and loves the airline as long as it runs on time. She is who the team could break.
**Confidence:** HIGH
**Based on:** 58 feedback items (positive or no-issue records across reviews, social posts, survey comments, and account notes)

## Identity
- **Age:** 28-38
- **Location:** Porto, Portugal
- **Occupation:** Architect
- **Bio:** Sofia flies five to eight times a year, a mix of work trips and city breaks, and has never had a cancellation with AnyCompany Air. She books on the app, uses the mobile boarding pass, and recommends the airline for punctuality. She would like a nicer seat preview but does not need one.

## Goals & Motivations
- **Primary Goal:** A fast, predictable trip from booking to landing.
- **Secondary Goals:** Choose a good seat; keep the app simple.
- **Definition of Success:** "Easy from booking to landing."
- **Deeper Motivation:** Low effort and no surprises.

## Pain Points
- **Challenges:** Few today. Minor annoyances: upsell steps, seat preview [Known].
- **Blockers:** None.
- **Workarounds:** None needed.
- **Emotional Impact:** Satisfied and loyal, but only while flights run on time.

## Behavior
- **Current Solutions:** App for booking, check-in, and boarding pass.
- **Tools:** AnyCompany Air app, mobile boarding pass.
- **Frequency:** Monthly or less
- **Tech Affinity:** High
- **Decision Style:** Gut feeling, based on past experience.

## Context & Environment
- **Usage Context:** At home when booking; at the airport for boarding.
- **Devices:** Smartphone.
- **Time Pressure:** Low when booking; she values speed.
- **Influencers:** Friends; her own track record with the airline.

## Quotes
> "AnyCompany Air on time again. Five for five this year" (frequent flyer, social)
> "Booked in 4 minutes on the app, honestly painless" (leisure traveller, social)
> "Easy app, mobile boarding pass worked first time, on time departure." (corporate traveller, review)
> "Honestly when AnyCompany Air runs on time it's my favourite airline" (leisure traveller, social)
> "Great flight. A 3D seat map would make it perfect." (leisure traveller, survey)

## Scenario
**A normal trip**
Sofia books a weekend in Vienna on the app in four minutes, checks in the day before, and walks to the gate with her phone. Anything that adds steps, notifications, or confusion to that path would annoy her. If she ever is disrupted, she expects the same app to sort it out as quickly as it booked her.

## Communication Style
- **Tone:** Casual, warm
- **Speech Patterns:** Short and upbeat: "painless", "smooth", "on time again".
- **Frustration Signals:** Mild: "could be simpler".
- **Enthusiasm Signals:** Public shout-outs to crew and punctuality.

## What the team could break for Sofia
- A slower booking or check-in path if disruption features crowd the app. [Assumed]
- Unwanted disruption notifications or prompts on trips that run on time. [Assumed]
