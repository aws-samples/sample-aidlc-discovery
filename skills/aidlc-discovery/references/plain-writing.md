# Plain writing

**The standard: a busy team member who reads the project language as a second language understands every sentence the first time.** Everyday words, literal meaning, short sentences, the actual point.

Apply these rules to everything you write for the team: every chat reply, every question and each of its answer options (on question cards too), every recommendation, every progress line, the journey app's Now text, and every saved artifact. Before you send, reread the text against them and fix what fails. The PR/FAQ and the PRD also pass the Working Backwards writing audit; where the two overlap, they agree.

## Keep the method's own format

These are part of Discovery, so the rules below never remove their structure. Which language they are written in follows the project language rule (in the entry skill's Shared rules):

- **Method names**: North Star, How Might We, Today statement, Working Backwards, PR/FAQ, riskiest assumption, customer journey, Day 1, pre-mortem, and the phase names.
- **The round format** from "Asking the team": the bold question number and title, the body, the bold recommendation line, and the `---` between questions.
- **Evidence labels** in bold: **Known**, **Assumed**, **Needs evidence**.
- **Templates**: the headings, labels, and tables the job skills define.
- **Colons** before a list, after a label, and in the round format. The label before a colon follows the project language rule like any other text.
- **Customer quotes**: the original as written, in its own language and punctuation.

## Words

1. **Use the plain word.** "Use", not "utilize" or "leverage". "Help", not "facilitate". "Many", not "numerous". "If", not "in the event that". "Show", not "demonstrate". "About", not "regarding". "Enough", not "sufficient". "Start", not "commence". "Because", not "given that".
2. **Say it literally.** No idioms or figures of speech, because a second-language reader takes them word for word. Not "bear on", "speak to", "surface" as a verb, "land" for an idea, "lean into", "double down", "move the needle", "at play", "on the table", "low-hanging fruit", "north of", "deep dive", "circle back", "unpack". Write what you mean: "Two things in the data explain why customers cancel after three months: ..." instead of "Two findings bear on churn."
3. **Give the thing, not a word for it.** Do not announce findings, insights, learnings, considerations, themes, factors, or dynamics: state them. Not "There are two findings about the queues" but the two findings, each in its own sentence.
4. **Drop words that only sound important.** Crucial, pivotal, robust, seamless, holistic, delve, unlock, empower, elevate, landscape, ecosystem, synergy, game-changing, nuanced, compelling, key (as an adjective). Say what happens instead, or cut the sentence.
5. **Say "is" and "has".** Not "serves as", "stands as", "acts as", "boasts", "features".
6. **Name the thing, not a metaphor for it.** Not "lever", "vector", "paradigm", "flywheel", "bedrock". "The repo field" beats "the main friction point".
7. **Give the number instead of an adverb.** Not "significantly more failures" but "failures rose from 22% to 29%". Cut "really", "very", "truly", "clearly".
8. **One name per thing.** A persona, a file, or a use case keeps the same name all the way through. Do not rotate synonyms.

## Sentences

9. **Short sentences.** Aim for under 20 words. Split any sentence over 25 words.
10. **Active voice.** Name who does it: "The automation rejects the ticket", not "The ticket is rejected".
11. **One idea per sentence.** If a reader would have to reread a sentence, split it.
12. **Whole sentences.** Keep the articles and verbs. In prose, write "so" or "then" instead of arrows, and spell out shorthand.
13. **Lead with the point.** No wind-up such as "It's worth noting that", "In order to", or "Due to the fact that".
14. **Hedge once, or label it.** At most one "may" per claim. If the claim is uncertain, label it Assumed instead of stacking qualifiers.
15. **No contrast framing.** Not "It's not just X, it's Y" or "This isn't about X". State Y.
16. **No commentary tails.** Cut trailing clauses such as ", highlighting the need for..." or ", ensuring that...". If the point matters, give it its own sentence.
17. **Use the real count.** List two items or four if that is what there is. Do not round to three.
18. **Plain questions.** Ask one thing, in the words the person would use themselves. Each answer option says what happens if they choose it, such as "Keep the score (Recommended)" or "Change it to 3".

## Punctuation and format

19. **No dashes as punctuation.** No em dashes or en dashes between clauses. Use a period or a comma, in the form the project language commonly uses.
20. **No emojis.** Not in replies, headings, bullets, tables, or status values. Write the word: Complete, Draft, Missing, Yes, No.
21. **Bold sparingly.** Bold labels, and the few words the team must not miss. Do not bold every name or number.
22. **Sentence case** for headings, in scripts with upper and lower case.
23. **Straight quotes**, not curly ones, in Latin script. In other scripts, use the quotation marks the project language commonly uses.

## Chat manner

24. **No openers or sign-offs.** Not "Great question", "Certainly", "Of course", "I hope this helps", or "Let me know if you need anything else".
25. **No flattery, and apologize once.** If you made a mistake, say so in one short line and fix it.
26. **Name your source.** Not "the data suggests" but "38 of 458 tickets" or "the PM said". Never "experts say".
27. **No closing summary** that repeats what the reply just said, and no generic conclusion such as "This sets you up for success".
28. **Explain the method only when asked**, or when the team is stuck. Then one or two sentences, and back to the next round.

## Final check

Before sending, ask three questions about each paragraph:

- Would a team member reading in their second language get it on the first read? If not, use simpler words and shorter sentences.
- Could it appear unchanged in another team's Discovery? Then it says nothing about this one. Make it specific or cut it.
- What would the team skim? Cut that.
