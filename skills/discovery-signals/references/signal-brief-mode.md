# Signal Brief mode

Use when the team brings Signal Briefs instead of raw data. A Signal Brief is a one-page, anonymized summary of one customer problem, written by the team in its own environment so the raw data never leaves it.

## What a brief contains

| Field | Content |
|-------|---------|
| Theme | The problem in one sentence |
| Job or desired outcome | What the customer is trying to achieve |
| Evidence | 2 to 4 anonymized quotes or observations, with volume and source |
| Estimated impact | Time, money, risk, or retention, even approximate |
| Confidence | High, medium, or low, and why |
| Segment | Who is affected, without personal data |
| Suggested next step | What to test or learn |

## Steps

1. **Read every brief** and check it is complete. List missing fields per brief; do not invent them.
2. **Check for personal data.** If a brief contains names, emails, company names, or account IDs, stop and ask the team to remove them before you continue.
3. **Merge overlapping briefs.** Two briefs describe the same problem when they share a root cause, even if they come from different teams, describe different segments, or name a different symptom: members calling about claim status, brokers fielding the same questions, and a low app rating about claim information are one problem. Ask of each pair: "would one fix solve both?" If yes, merge them. Keep the clearer theme, combine the evidence and the segments, and record which briefs were merged. Never list a symptom of a merged problem as its own signal; give it as evidence under the problem.
4. **Cross-check the evidence.** For each merged problem, note how many briefs and how many independent sources support it. A problem seen in three sources is stronger than one seen in one.
5. **Score frequency, severity, and reach** from the briefs' volumes, impact, and segments. Frequency comes from the stated volumes, not from the number of briefs, because one person may write several briefs about the same problem.
6. **Write the Signal Analysis Report** in the same format as for raw data. In the data line, give the number of briefs and the number of authors.

## Confidence

Keep each brief's own confidence rating, and lower it when the evidence is a single source or the volume is an estimate. Label claims that come only from a brief's impact estimate as Assumed.

## Link to Working Backwards

Each problem in the report is most of a candidate use case: the theme and segment become the problem and who has it, and the evidence carries over. Note the brief IDs against each signal so the scoring can cite them.
