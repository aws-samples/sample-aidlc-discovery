# Writing audit

Run on the PR/FAQ and the PRD before showing them to the team, and after every rewrite. Fix what fails, then tell the team in one line what you checked and what you changed.

## Voice
1. **The customer is the subject.** "Chefs can now confirm…", not "[Company] launches…" or "The platform enables…".
2. **Active voice.** "The app shows the delivery date", not "The delivery date is shown".
3. **Third person in the press release.** "You" only inside quotes.
4. **Positive first sentences.** No paragraph opens with "not", "doesn't", "isn't", or "won't". Say what the product does; objections go in the FAQ.

## Words
5. **No superlatives or buzzwords:** revolutionary, game-changing, seamless, easy, simple, cutting-edge, world-class, best-in-class.
6. **No unproven firsts:** "first", "first-ever", or "first of its kind" only with evidence.
7. **No em dashes.** Use commas, colons, or a new sentence.
8. **Specific numbers** instead of adjectives, each with its evidence label.

## Structure
9. **One idea per bullet or sentence.** Separate personas, problems, and solutions get separate treatment.
10. **Committed, not hedged.** Personas have one age and one name; targets have one number. If you cannot decide, ask the team.
11. **Nothing invented.** Every fact traces to the signals, the personas, or the team. Otherwise label it Assumed or Needs evidence, or remove it.
12. **Claims keep their scope.** A figure from one account, one quote, or one segment is presented as that, not as true of every customer.
13. **One set of targets.** The PR/FAQ, the PRD, and the success criteria use the North Star's numbers, or say why one differs.
14. **Light labels in the press release.** No "[Known]" there; Assumed and Needs evidence once each, where they apply.

## Content that never belongs in the team's documents
15. **No commercial interest of a vendor,** no competitor positioning of vendors, and no internal strategy of anyone outside the team. The documents describe the customer's problem and the customer's outcome.
