Reference · AI-DLC Discovery

Phase map: every stage, what it asks, what it does

One page per stage, for facilitators and for slides. Each stage says its question, its skill, what it needs, its steps, what it leaves behind, its checkpoint, and where it goes wrong.

Sources: the entry skill, each job skill under skills/, and the run-sheet. Terms follow the glossary. Examples come from the unattended Air run (reference-runs/anycompany-air/); the run facilitated with a team (anycompany-air-with-team/) shows the team's decisions and is the one train-the-trainer demos. When a skill changes, check this sheet against it.

The rules that run through every stage

  1. The team decides. The assistant drafts, cites, and asks. No score, ranking, or decision is final until the team confirms it. Every stage ends at a checkpoint.
  2. Evidence labels. Every claim in the PR/FAQ, PRD, GTM section, and brief is Known (backed by the data), Assumed (believed, not shown), or Needs evidence (it matters to the decision and must be tested).
  3. Anonymized. No names, emails, companies, or account identifiers in quotes. Raw data stays in discovery/data/, which is never committed.
  4. No architecture. Discovery decides who, what, and why. Technology choices belong to the build.
  5. Files, not chat. Every artifact is saved under discovery/[project]/ as soon as it exists. With a team Space, that path is in the Space.
  6. One step at a time, with the progress board at every checkpoint, and three lists carried forward: what we know, what we don't know, what we're assuming.
  7. The customer's interest only. No artifact mentions the facilitator's company's commercial interest.

Before the day · optional

Signal Briefs

How does a team bring its evidence without bringing its data?

When data can't leave the building, the team writes one anonymized page per customer problem, and brings only those.

Skill
discovery-signal-brief, run by the team in its own environment, days before.
Steps
Find the evidence → measure volumes as aggregates → write the brief (seven fields: theme, job, anonymized evidence with volumes, impact, confidence, segment, next step) → anonymization check, then the team reads it once more.
Leaves
discovery/signal-briefs/[problem].md, one per problem.
Quality bar
Someone who never saw the raw data could write the first paragraph of a press release from the brief alone.

Used by the AnyCompany Health sample, where the data never leaves the insurer. The other two inputs are production data (the default) and a synthetic dataset prepared before the day; the assistant never invents data on the day.

Any stage · optional · AWS Quick only

Team Space

Where is the workshop saved, and who writes to it?

Save the workshop in a team Space, and everyone can follow it, open the prototype, and take over from their own AWS Quick. One driver at a time.

Skill
discovery-team-space. On when storage: space is set in the config, or when the team asks at kickoff ("save the workshop in our team Space"). With a Space attached and storage empty, it asks once: "Save the workshop in the [space] Space, or in this folder?" Design: docs/team-space.md.
Before the day
The team creates and names the Space, shares it as Owners with everyone who may follow or take over, and each person attaches it to their chat (the + menu, then Spaces). The skill test-writes PROJECT.md, reads it back, and adds Storage and Driver lines.
What changes
Only where files are read from and written to. The phases, checkpoints, files, and paths stay the same.
One driver
Saving replaces a file, so before every write the skill reads PROJECT.md. If someone else is driving, it stops: "[Driver] is driving this workshop since [time]. Take over?" Everyone else reads, asks questions, and opens the prototype. Owners can take over; Viewers read.
Customer data
Everyone a Space is shared with can read it. Sample data is fictional and can go in. Real data goes in the team Space only if nobody who shouldn't see it has access: the team confirms, and PROJECT.md records it. Otherwise it stays local, or in a separate restricted Space named in the start prompt.
Take over
"Continue the AI-DLC Discovery workshop for [project] from discovery/[project]/PROJECT.md in the [space] space. I'm taking over as [role]. Start at [step]." The previous driver stops writing.
Pods
One project per pod in the same Space, discovery/[project]-[pod]/, each with its own driver, created after scoring. The facilitator sees every pod.
Goes wrong
Two people wrote at once: the last save wins, so compare what each changed and redo the lost change as the agreed driver. The Space isn't attached: saves fail or land locally. A Viewer tries to drive: make them an Owner, or let an Owner drive.

Lesson: 0009 Running in a team Space.

Stage 0 · 09:00 · 30 min

Setup and kickoff

What are we here to move, and with which data?

Agree the product, the people, the one business metric and its baseline, and where the evidence comes from.

Skill
aidlc-discovery, Step 1.
Confirms, in order
The product and who uses it; who is in the room and whether they split into pods; the business metric with its current value; the data input (production, signal-briefs, synthetic) and where it is; the engagement reference (never a CRM or account identifier).
Where it's saved
A local working folder, or a team Space (recommended in AWS Quick; see above). The start prompt names it: "Start an AI-DLC Discovery workshop. Project: [name]. Engagement reference: [reference or Self-serve]. Save the workshop in the [Space name] space. Data: [input], in [folder]." Leave out the Space sentence for a local folder.
Leaves
discovery/[project]/PROJECT.md: scope, choices, phase status (and, with a Space, Storage and Driver).
Good looks like
It drafts the setup from what it can find and asks the room to confirm. The product owner confirms the business metric aloud, because everything is scored against it.
Facilitator move
Don't confirm the setup yourself; ask the product owner "Is anything here wrong or missing?" If the sponsor leaves after kickoff, ask them now whether this is the metric they care about.
Goes wrong
The data doesn't match the product. It must stop and offer real data, Signal Briefs, or a prepared dataset, and never write data to fill the gap.

Stage 1 · 09:30 · 60 min

Signals

What are customers telling us?

The team writes down what it believes first; then the data shows which beliefs hold, with counts and customer quotes.

Skill
discovery-signals
Needs
The data from Setup.
  1. Team expectations, before any analysis. At least three, each a sentence the data could prove wrong, written down as given. AI analysis is persuasive; with beliefs on record, the team can check itself.
  2. Find and check the data. An inventory: sources, counts, period, gaps. The team confirms it is what it meant to bring.
  3. Analyze. Per record: sentiment, category, urgency, root cause, persona signal, quote; aggregated with Python. Signal Briefs are merged and cross-checked instead.
  4. Signal Analysis Report. Three to seven top signals scored on frequency, severity, and reach (out of 30), each with who, evidence, quotes, and a root-cause hypothesis; a "What the team expected" table; five customer voices; data gaps.
  5. Hypotheses. Five to ten, each with evidence, confidence, and a test, including the team's expectations that the data didn't settle.
  6. Review. The top three signals, five full quotes read aloud, then: what contradicts this, and what is under-represented?
Leaves
signals/team-expectations.md, signal-summary.md, hypotheses.md
Checkpoint
"Here are the top signals and their evidence. What do you challenge before we decide what to work on?"
Principle
Weigh signal over frequency: severity and reach count as much as volume. In AnyCompany Air, "Delayed baggage" is the top single call reason, but disruption is split across six reasons and is 42 of 110 calls.
Facilitator moves
Ask the quietest person first. Before confirming the inventory, ask the person closest to each gap whether that data exists. With a contradicted belief, read what the person got right first. Ask "Where does it work well?" before the challenge. Close by crediting challengers by name and getting a yes from the product owner.
Goes wrong
It skips the expectations or jumps to verdicts: stop it and restart the phase at Step 1.

Stage 2a · 10:45 · 75 min

Working Backwards I: personas and scoring

Which problem first, and for whom?

Put faces on the signals, then score 3 to 5 problems on six weighted dimensions. The team confirms every score and picks one.

Personas

Skill
discovery-personas, mode generate.
Does
Finds behavioural segments in the feedback and writes three or more personas, one of them a customer who is satisfied today, so the team sees what it could break. Every quote is real; each persona has a confidence rating and an evidence count. Synthetic data or Signal Briefs cap confidence at Medium.
Check
"Have you met this person?" Any persona the team doesn't recognise is changed or dropped. Overlaps go to set-aside-personas.md.
Leaves
working-backwards/personas/[name].md

Use case scoring

Skill
discovery-use-case-scoring
Candidates
3 to 5 problems, not features, each with five fields: who, problem, evidence, business metric, owner in the room.
Weights
Fixed before the first score: pain 25, outcome 25, reach 15, fit 10, feasibility 15, adoption load 10. The sponsor may move one by up to 10 points.
Gate
Merge duplicates first, then drop any candidate failing: evidence, outcome, owner, build path, or painkiller ("would customers notice if it never shipped?").
Score
1 to 5 on each dimension, each with one line of evidence. Business outcome is scored against the metric agreed at setup. Weighted score = Σ(score × weight) ÷ 100.
Confidence
High: three or more independent signals incl. one quantitative. Medium: two, or one strong quantitative. Low: one source, or an assumption.
Decision
≥ 3.5 and High → build first. ≥ 3.5 and Medium/Low → prototype to learn. 2.5–3.4 → defer. < 2.5 → drop. Only one candidate per team (or pod) goes forward; others that qualify are "defer, next in line".
Leaves
working-backwards/use-case-scoring.md, with an Overrides table.
Checkpoint
Every score confirmed or overridden with a reason; one use case selected per pod.
Facilitator moves
Feasibility belongs to the engineering lead: "What would make this a 2, and what a 4?" Ask a candidate's owner before the room agrees. After an override, show whether the decision changed. "Agree with all" when the room agrees; spend time on the disagreements.

Lunch, 12:00. "Draft the PR/FAQ while we're away." The skill runs Working Backwards in draft mode and writes a straw man for the team to challenge.

Stage 2b · 13:00 · 45 min

Working Backwards II: from press release to riskiest assumption

How would we announce it, and what must the prototype prove?

Write the press release before anything is built. If the benefit can't be announced clearly, don't build it.

Skills
discovery-working-backwards, then discovery-gtm in draft mode.
Needs
The selected use case, the Signal Analysis Report, the personas, the scoring.
  1. The five questions, one at a time: Listen (who, insights, pain) · Define (the problem; framing rules) · Invent (solutions, capabilities, differentiator) · Refine · Test. Define produces three sentences:
    • Today statement: "Today, [persona] have to [problem] when [context]. They need a way to [outcome, never a mechanism]."
    • How Might We: "How might we [outcome] for [persona] so that [benefit]?"
    • North Star: "Deploy [capability, not a product name] by [when] to [metric 1] and [metric 2], in order to [goal]." Its numbers are the day's targets.
  2. Feature description. 3 to 5 feature ideas; the team chooses or combines one.
  3. PR/FAQ. Headline, subheading, exactly seven paragraphs (summary, problem, solution, leader quote, customer experience, testimonial, call to action), 500 to 700 words, no headings inside. Then 5–7 customer FAQs and 5–7 internal FAQs. The testimonial is from a persona, labelled synthetic. A writing audit runs before the team sees it. The team reads it aloud and changes what doesn't sound like them.
  4. Readiness check. Seven questions: persona, pain today, prototype experience (the slice), vision, views and signals, data, prototype metric. GREEN none missing · AMBER 1–3 gaps, carry as open hypotheses · RED 4+, fix the PR/FAQ before prototyping.
  5. PRD. P0, P1, P2. P0 small enough to prototype in an hour. No architecture.
  6. Riskiest assumption. From the Assumed and Needs evidence claims: the one that, if wrong, ends the idea; what the prototype must show; how Validate will judge it.
  7. GTM draft (about 10 min): first adopters with 2–3 design partners, a positioning line that names the main alternative (including doing nothing), business model and launch plan as hypotheses.
Leaves
working-backwards.md, feature-description.md, prfaq.md (with readiness check and Prototype scope), prd.md, riskiest-assumption.md, gtm.md
Checkpoint
"Here is what you chose, why, and what the prototype must prove. Ready to prototype?"
Facilitator moves
Listen for the framing first: an outcome in "need a way to", a capability in the North Star. AMBER is a good result; don't chase GREEN. For the riskiest assumption, everyone chooses before anyone speaks. Set the pass mark now, before any result. Put a name and date on every open question that blocks the build. Targets are said aloud by the people who own them.
Goes wrong
The press release reads like a vision document (headings, > 700 words, paragraphs doing two jobs): ask for seven paragraphs, one job each, and the writing audit. The team accepts the draft unchanged: have it read aloud.

Stage 3 · 13:45 · 60 min

Prototype

What would it look like?

A clickable, multi-screen prototype aimed at the riskiest assumption, critiqued by the personas, and changed at least once.

Skills
discovery-prototype, which calls discovery-personas in critique mode.
Needs
prd.md and riskiest-assumption.md.
  1. Plan the screens. 4 to 8: entry, main task steps, confirmation, the main unhappy path. One screen is marked the test moment, where the riskiest assumption becomes visible.
  2. Build from the shell: one self-contained HTML file with Flow, Mobile, and Desktop views. Only the region between the edit markers changes. Opens on the Flow board.
  3. Team click-through as the main persona. Note reactions; don't change anything yet.
  4. Persona critique. Each persona walks the core flow in its own voice and gives a day-one adoption score with a reason. It usually finds what the team missed.
  5. Iterate. The team picks which findings to fix. Every iteration is a new version file, never an overwrite, logged with the finding behind each change. A fix that promises an unconfirmed capability is logged as Needs evidence.
Leaves
prototype/v1.html, v2.html…, index.html (current), versions.md, compare.html, persona-critique.md, notes.md
Checkpoint
"Here is what your personas said and what we changed. Ready to try to break it?"
Facilitator moves
When the assistant changes the agreed plan, read each change and ask its owner "Keep it, or change it back?" Mark claims on screen nobody has confirmed as Needs evidence. "Looks good" at the first click-through is fine; the critique finds the problems. When the assistant asks a factual question (what time does the line close?), get the answer from the person who knows, or mark it Needs evidence. Fix first what touches the riskiest assumption, then what is cheap; if a finding changes what the assumption means, update it now.
Goes wrong
One plain page: it didn't build from the shell. Looks finished but tests nothing: go back to the test moment. The team iterates on colours: ask what the persona would trip over. The compare page shows empty frames in AWS Quick: open compare.html from the working folder in a browser.

Stage 4 · 15:00 · 45 min

Validate

What could make it fail, and how will we know it worked?

Try to prove it fails with what's in the room, set measurable success criteria, then decide: commit, pivot, or park.

Skills
discovery-validate (review, then decide), discovery-gtm (complete), and discovery-use-case-scoring (rescore) if the review changed a score.
Needs
prototype/index.html and persona-critique.md.
  1. Re-walk the iterated prototype with the primary personas, only for what changed (5 min). The critique judged v1; Validate judges the version handed off.
  2. Four risks, after Cagan: valuable (will they change what they do?), usable (can each persona finish the flow unaided?), feasible (is there a known path? unknowns, not architecture), viable (does it work for the business?). Each: assumption, evidence for and against, verdict (supported, contradicted, open), the test that would settle it. The riskiest assumption is checked explicitly.
  3. Pre-mortem, after Klein: "It's 90 days after launch and it failed. Why?" Everyone writes a reason before anyone speaks, and the round starts with the most optimistic person; the assistant adds its own after the room; five kept, each with an early warning sign and a prevention step.
  4. Success criteria. 3 to 5, each with metric, baseline, target, and when checked (30, 60, 90 days), consistent with the North Star.
  5. Prioritize the problems by frequency, severity, reach, with the open hypotheses listed.
  6. GTM complete: viability check (legal, privacy, sales, support, marketing, each with an owner role) and the adoption and success table, with targets and kill criteria.
  7. Decision. Pick the prototype version to hand off. Then commit (build it; if the riskiest assumption is still open, commit to build or to test first, with conditions, owners, and dates), pivot (reframe, next use case at 30 days), or park (stop, keep the evidence, say what would reopen it).
Leaves
validate/risk-review.md, prioritization.md, decision.md; gtm.md completed.
Checkpoint
"Commit, pivot, or park, and why?"
Principle
Never wait for surveys. What can't be settled today stays an open hypothesis and goes in the brief. A validated decision not to build is a good outcome.
Facilitator moves
A condition needs four things before the room moves on: what is measured, the threshold, who checks it, and by which follow-up, consistent with what was agreed in Working Backwards. A decision given before the review is finished is saved as the team's intent, with what is still open; that is correct.
Example
AnyCompany Air reference run: commit, testing the riskiest assumption and feasibility first, with three conditions, each with an owner, due by the 30-day follow-up (decision.md).
Goes wrong
Every risk comes back supported: ask what the satisfied persona loses. The team wants to skip Validate: offer the 20-minute version, four risks and the decision.

Stage 5 · 15:45 · 30 min

Handoff

What does the build start from?

One Markdown brief, written for someone who wasn't in the room: the decision, the evidence, and what is still unproven.

Skill
discovery-handoff
Needs
Every artifact above and validate/decision.md. It never writes a partial brief.
  1. Check the required artifacts. Thirteen files (fourteen with a current-product map); anything missing is listed with the skill that makes it.
  2. Write the brief, 15 sections, under 200,000 characters, summaries plus links: engagement reference · decision · problem and evidence · personas · prioritized use cases · PR/FAQ summary · success criteria · GTM · scope · prioritized backlog · prototype screens and flows · current product · constraints · open hypotheses · full artifacts. No architecture.
  3. AI-DLC team knowledge (when the route is AI-DLC): the personas in aidlc/spaces/[space]/knowledge/aidlc-product-agent/. Adds discovery/data/ to .gitignore.
  4. Next steps. For commit with AI-DLC 2.10.0+, it prints the start command (/aidlc workshop or /aidlc classic) and does not run it. For pivot or park, what happens at 30 days.
Leaves
handoff/discovery-brief.md, handoff/next-steps.md
Facilitator move
Before the brief, confirm, change, or label as a proposal everything the assistant drafted on the team's behalf (the reason, interim targets, kill criteria, owners). The brief carries it to engineering as if the team had decided it.
In a team Space
No .gitignore, because there is no repository. Next steps say to download discovery/[project]/ and aidlc/ from the Space into the build repository, never the data. aidlc/spaces/ is AI-DLC's own folder layout, not an AWS Quick Space.
Checkpoint
"Could an engineer who wasn't here start from this brief?"
Why it matters
When a team continues into AI-DLC, the brief replaces AI-DLC's own Ideation phase, and AI-DLC starts at Inception. The brief is written for every decision, including park.

16:15 and after

Readout and follow-ups

Did it work, and what did we learn?

The sponsor hears the decision; real users test what the personas only simulated; the numbers are checked at 30 and 60 days.

Readout (45 min)
Each pod shows the decision, the prototype on the Flow view, the success criteria, and the next step with its owner. Under five minutes each. When the sponsor asks "why not do what I believed?", answer with the evidence from Signals, then bring it back to the next step.
7 days
Baseline for the business metric in; brief in the build repository; owners in next-steps.md started.
By 30 days
Real users try the prototype (the personas are synthetic); open hypotheses go to real customers, with discovery-survey if useful; deferred use cases are re-scored.
30 and 60 days
Adoption and the business metric against the success and kill criteria.
Loop
What the team learns goes back into Signals for the next cycle.
