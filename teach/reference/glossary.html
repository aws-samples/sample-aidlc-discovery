Reference · AI-DLC Discovery

Glossary

The method's own words, as the skills use them. Every lesson and slide follows these.

Structure

Phase
One of the five stages: Signals, Working Backwards, Prototype, Validate, Handoff. Setup comes before them.
Step
A numbered unit of work inside a skill. Each has an input, an output, a validation, and what to do on failure.
Skill
One job, packaged as a folder with a SKILL.md. The entry skill, aidlc-discovery, runs eleven job skills; the build bundles them into one installable skill with a module per job.
Checkpoint
The stop at the end of every phase, where the team reviews and decides before anything moves on.
Artifact
A file saved under discovery/[project]/. Later phases read artifacts, not the chat.
Progress board
Two tables (phases, and artifacts in this phase) shown at every checkpoint, plus three lists carried forward: what we know, what we don't know, what we're assuming.
Pod
A sub-team of 4 to 5 that takes one selected use case from scoring onward. Two pods never take the same one.
Unattended run
A run with no checkpoints, for automated tests and demonstrations only (unattended: true).

Team Space (AWS Quick, optional)

Storage
Where the workshop is saved: local (a working folder, the default) or space (an AWS Quick team Space).
Team Space
An AWS Quick Space holding the same discovery/[project]/ files, so everyone it's shared with can follow, open the prototype, and take over. Not to be confused with aidlc/spaces/, which is AI-DLC's own folder layout.
Driver
The one person writing the workshop now, named by role or first name in PROJECT.md. Checked before every write.
Take over
A new driver continues from PROJECT.md in the Space with the take-over prompt; the previous driver stops writing.
Owner · Viewer
Space roles. Owners can write and take over; Viewers read, ask questions, and open the prototype.

Evidence

Known
Backed by data from Signals: a count, quote, or metric.
Assumed
Believed, not shown. The riskiest assumption is chosen from these.
Needs evidence
Matters to the decision and has to be tested.
Data input
production (the default), signal-briefs, or synthetic (prepared before the day). Never data written during the workshop.
Signal Brief
A one-page anonymized summary of one customer problem, written by the team in its own environment.
Team hypotheses
What the team expects the data to show, written down before any analysis.
Signal Analysis Report
signal-summary.md: top signals scored on frequency, severity, and reach, with quotes, the "What the team expected" table, and data gaps.
Persona
A synthetic customer drawn from the data, with real quotes and a confidence rating. A hypothesis, not customer evidence. One is always satisfied today.

Choosing

Use case
A candidate written as a problem, with who, problem, evidence, business metric, and owner.
Gate
Five pass/fail tests before scoring: evidence, outcome, owner, build path, painkiller.
Weighted score
Six 1-to-5 scores (pain, outcome, reach, fit, feasibility, adoption load) times their weights, divided by 100.
Build first · Prototype to learn · Defer · Drop
The scoring decisions. Only one candidate per team or pod is taken forward.
Override
A team change to a drafted score, recorded with its reason.

Working Backwards

Five questions
Listen, Define, Invent, Refine, Test, asked one at a time.
End customer
The person whose day changes when this ships. Not the team, not the sponsor.
Today statement
"Today, [persona] have to [problem] when [context]. They need a way to [outcome]." The outcome is never a mechanism.
How Might We
One question that opens the solution space: outcome, persona, benefit.
North Star
"Deploy [capability] by [when] to [metrics], in order to [goal]." Its numbers are the day's targets.
PR/FAQ
The press release (headline, subheading, seven paragraphs, 500 to 700 words) plus customer and internal FAQs.
Writing audit
A check run on the PR/FAQ and PRD before the team sees them.
Readiness check
Seven questions on the PR/FAQ; verdict GREEN, AMBER (1–3 gaps), or RED (4+).
Prototype slice
The part of the full journey the prototype will show.
PRD
Requirements ranked P0, P1, P2. P0 fits a one-hour prototype.
Riskiest assumption
The one Assumed claim that, if wrong, ends the idea, with what the prototype must show and how Validate will judge it.
GTM section
Seven parts: first adopters, positioning, business model, viability check, launch plan, adoption and success, risks. Drafted in Working Backwards, completed in Validate.

Prototype and Validate

Test moment
The screen where the riskiest assumption becomes visible.
Shell
The fixed HTML that gives every prototype its Flow, Mobile, and Desktop views. Only the region between the edit markers changes.
Version
v1.html, v2.html… never overwritten; index.html is the current or chosen one.
Persona critique
Each persona walks the prototype in its own voice and gives a day-one adoption score.
Re-walk
Validate's short check that the iteration fixed what the critique found.
Four risks
Valuable, usable, feasible, viable. Each verdict is supported, contradicted, or open.
Pre-mortem
"It's 90 days after launch and it failed. Why?"
Kill criteria
A specific result by a specific date that means the team stops.
Commit · Pivot · Park
The decision. Commit can mean "test first", with conditions before the build.
Open hypothesis
Anything Validate could not settle. It goes into the brief as a question for the build.

Handoff

Discovery brief
The one Markdown document the build starts from: 15 sections, under 200,000 characters, no architecture.
Route
Own engineering, agents, AI-DLC workshop, AI-DLC classic, or none.
AI-DLC
The AI-Driven Development Lifecycle. Discovery's brief replaces its Ideation phase; AI-DLC starts at Inception.
Follow-ups
7 days (baseline, brief in the repository), 30 days (real users, open hypotheses), 30 and 60 days (metrics against targets).
