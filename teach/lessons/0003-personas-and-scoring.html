Lesson 0003 · About 18 minutes

Personas and scoring

Working Backwards, first half: put faces on the signals, then choose one problem in a way the team owns.

Your winRun a scoring session: apply the gate, work out a weighted score and its decision by hand, and keep each score owned by the person who should own it. This is the most contested 75 minutes of the day.

Personas come first, because everything after refers to them

Scoring, the PR/FAQ, the prototype, and the critique all talk about specific people, so discovery-personas runs before scoring.discovery-personas/SKILL.md, Step 1. It finds behavioural segments in the feedback and writes three or more personas. Every quote is real, and each persona has a confidence rating and an evidence count.

  • One is satisfied today, so the team sees what it could break. In Air that's Hanne, a happy on-time flyer, built from 48 positive records, High confidence.personas/hanne.md
  • Confidence is capped at Medium for a synthetic dataset, and for Signal Briefs unless three or more briefs describe the same segment.
  • Names, ages, and backgrounds vary, and are never paired with roles by stereotype.
  • They are hypotheses, not customers. A persona's reaction is something to test with real users later.

Your move: ask the people closest to customers, "Have you met this person?" Change or drop any persona the room doesn't recognise. Every persona costs time in the critique, so ask whether any two overlap. Overlaps go to set-aside-personas.md, never into personas/.

Scoring: problems, not features

The team brings 3 to 5 candidates, each written as a problem with five fields: who has it, the problem, the evidence, the business metric it moves, and the owner in the room. If someone brings a feature ("a 3D seat map"), ask what problem it solves, and for whom.discovery-use-case-scoring/SKILL.md

1. Weights, fixed before the first score

Pain 25 · Outcome 25 · Reach 15 · Fit 10 · Feasibility 15 · Adoption load 10. The sponsor may move a weight by up to 10 points, but only now. Tell the room why: "Once we see scores, nobody can move a weight to help their favourite."

2. The gate

First merge duplicates: a candidate that is a symptom of another's problem, or one solution to it, joins that candidate. Then drop any candidate that fails one of five tests:

  1. Evidence: Signals shows customer evidence for the problem.
  2. Outcome: it links to a business metric the customer cares about.
  3. Owner: someone in the room owns the build.
  4. Build path: there is a path to a build.
  5. Painkiller: customers would notice if it never shipped.

Failing the gate is a drop, never a deferral. In Air, the 3D seat map fails on painkiller: every record comes from a happy customer saying "not a complaint" or "nice touch". It stays alive only as an open hypothesis with a cheap test: try seat photos on the existing map and watch paid seat selection.Reference run, use-case-scoring.md, Gate. When a stakeholder's favourite fails, that's the gate doing its job: the reason becomes visible.

3. Six scores, each with its evidence

DimensionWt15
Customer pain25Rare, nice to haveFrequent, blocking; customers pay for workarounds or leave
Business outcome25No measurable link to the agreed metricDirect movement within 90 days
Reach15A small nicheMost users, or the highest-value segment
Strategic fit or threat10Off strategyCore, or "do nothing" is costing customers now
Feasibility15New data, platform, skillsBuilds on what exists
Adoption load10Heavy change for usersFits how users already work

Outcome is the dimension that surprises rooms. It is scored against the metric agreed at setup, not the one a candidate names for itself. In Air the metric is the self-service rebooking rate. Bag tracking has real pain (4) but moves baggage calls, so it scores 1 on outcome. Strategic fit and feasibility are drafted, then marked for the team to confirm, because the data rarely shows them.

4. Weighted score, confidence, decision

Weighted score = Σ(score × weight) ÷ 100, from 1.0 to 5.0. Confidence: High is three or more independent signals including one quantitative source; Medium is two, or one strong quantitative source; Low is a single source, or the team's assumption.

WeightedConfidenceDecision
3.5 or moreHighBuild first
3.5 or moreMedium or LowPrototype to learn: a high-value idea is never scored out for low confidence
2.5 to 3.4AnyDefer: revisit at 30 days with new evidence
Below 2.5AnyDrop, and record why

One decision per team. Only the highest-scoring qualifier goes forward. Any other that qualifies becomes "defer, next in line", so the day has one focus. With pods, each pod takes one, and two pods never take the same one.

Running the room

  • Feasibility belongs to the engineering lead. Don't let a drafted 3 stand by silence: "What would make this a 2, and what would make it a 4?" The answer is the build's biggest unknown.
  • Ask the owner first. "It's your candidate. Anything the scores miss?" An owner who agrees out loud to defer their own idea is a stronger result than a silent defer.
  • After an override, show the new weighted score and say whether the decision changed. Often it doesn't, and the person has still been heard. In the Air run with a team, the head of digital raised the booking flow's pain to 4: the score moved from 2.50 to 3.00 and stayed Defer.anycompany-air-with-team/…/use-case-scoring.md Record every override with its reason, and label it Assumed when no evidence backs it.
  • "Agree with all" when the room agrees with a candidate's drafts. Spend the time on the scores people disagree with.
  • If every candidate lands between 3 and 4, the candidates are probably too broad. Ask the team to make them more specific.

Checkpoint: every score confirmed or overridden, with reasons, and one use case selected per pod. Then, at lunch: "Draft the PR/FAQ while we're away."run-sheet.md, 10:45 and 12:00.

For facilitators comparing runs: the Air answer key expects rebooking around 4.45 and the booking flow around 2.50; the unattended reference run scored 4.60 and 2.20, and the run with a team drafted the booking flow at 2.50. The decisions match where it matters: rebooking is build first, the seat map is dropped at the gate, and bag tracking is next in line. Expect some spread in individual scores and judge the run on the decisions.anycompany-air.md, section 4 (spoilers).

1. Why does the skill generate personas before scoring?
Scoring, the PR/FAQ, the prototype, and the critique all refer to the personas, so they come first. The gate tests the problem, not personas, and the signals stay the evidence.
2. Why is one persona always a customer who is satisfied today?
A change aimed at unhappy customers can hurt happy ones. In Validate, "what does the satisfied persona lose?" is the question to ask when every risk comes back supported.
3. Every 3D seat map record comes from a happy customer who'd "like it as a nice touch". What happens to the candidate?
Customers wouldn't notice if it never shipped, so it's dropped at the gate before scoring. Failing the gate is never a deferral; to keep it alive, record it as an open hypothesis with a cheap test.
4. Bag tracking has real pain, yet it scores 1 on business outcome. Why?
Outcome is scored against the metric agreed at setup, the self-service rebooking rate, not the metric a candidate brings for itself. Bag evidence is in all six sources, and feasibility is a separate dimension.
5. A team without pods has two candidates above 3.5, both with High confidence. What happens?
One decision per team: the highest weighted score goes forward, and the other becomes "defer, next in line". Pods are agreed at setup, not invented mid-scoring.
6. From lesson 0001: which file must exist before Working Backwards can start?
The Signal Analysis Report. Scoring draws its candidates and evidence from it, and personas are built from it and the data behind it.
7. The engineering lead says nothing about a drafted feasibility score of 3. What do you ask?
Asking for the 2 and the 4 brings out the build's biggest unknown without turning Discovery into architecture. A score that stands by silence isn't owned.
