Lesson 0009 · About 10 minutes · AWS Quick only

Running in a team Space

Same method, same files. Everyone follows from their own AWS Quick, and one person drives.

Your winSet up a workshop in a team Space before the day, keep the customer data where it belongs, and hand facilitation over mid-workshop without anyone's work being overwritten.

What it changes, and what it doesn't

By default a workshop lives in one working folder on one laptop, and the room watches a shared screen. In a team Space, the same discovery/[project]/ files live in an AWS Quick Space. Everyone it's shared with attaches it in their own AWS Quick and can read every artifact, ask questions about it ("what did the persona critique find?"), open the prototype, and take over facilitation.discovery-team-space/SKILL.md; design and verification notes in docs/team-space.md.

Nothing in the method changes: not the phases, the checkpoints, the files, or their paths. Only where the files are read from and written to. It's optional, and a local folder remains the default.

Before the day

  1. The team creates and names the Space (Spaces, then Create space). The assistant never invents one.
  2. It shares the Space as Owners with everyone who may follow or take over. Viewers can read, ask, and open the prototype, but shouldn't drive.
  3. Each person attaches it to their chat: the + menu, then Spaces.
  4. For a sample run, copy the sample into the Space first (the copy prompt in dry-run-air.md, step 0). Sample data is fictional, so it can go in.

The start prompt names it: "…Save the workshop in the [Space name] space. Data: [input], in [folder]." The skill test-writes PROJECT.md, reads it back word for word, and adds two lines: Storage and Driver. Tell the room AWS Quick may ask them to approve each save. Everyone else follows along in their own chat, reading, not writing.run-sheet.md, Before the day and 09:00 Kickoff.

One driver at a time

Saving a file to a Space replaces it. Two people writing the same workshop would overwrite each other, so before every write the skill:

  1. reads PROJECT.md from the Space, and if the driver is someone else, stops: "[Driver] is driving this workshop since [time]. Take over?";
  2. checks the text for personal data (real names, emails, phone numbers, account identifiers) and removes it;
  3. saves to the same path. Prototype versions stay safe because each one is a new vN.html.

Taking over is one prompt, in the new driver's own AWS Quick:

"Continue the AI-DLC Discovery workshop for [project] from discovery/[project]/PROJECT.md in the [space] space. I'm taking over as [role]. Start at [step]."

It shows who was driving and since when, asks the previous driver to stop writing, updates the driver line, and shows the progress board. If two people wrote at once anyway, the last save wins: ask both what they changed, re-read from the Space, and redo the lost change. Name the driver by role or first name, never an email.

In train-the-trainer, each team gets its own Space, and the second facilitator takes over from their own chat at the halfway point instead of driving by voice.train-the-trainer.md, Teams and 00:45.

The data stays out (usually)

Everything in a Space can be read and queried by everyone it's shared with. So:

  • Sample data is fictional and can go in the team Space.
  • Real customer data goes in only if the Space is shared with nobody who shouldn't see it. The skill asks the team to confirm before Signals and records the answer in PROJECT.md.
  • Otherwise the data stays in the driver's local discovery/data/, or in a separate restricted Space named in the start prompt. Only artifacts go in the team Space, and those already carry anonymized quotes only.

Pods, prototype, handoff

Pods
Each pod is its own project in the same Space, discovery/[project]-[pod]/, with its own driver, created after scoring. The shared Signals and scoring artifacts are copied into each pod's project. Pods never write to each other's files, and the facilitator sees all of them.
Prototype
Every version is saved to the Space and opens in the session tab from there, so anyone can click through it in their own AWS Quick. Opened from the Space in a browser, it downloads instead. compare.html shows empty frames in AWS Quick, Space or not: open it from a working folder in a browser.
Handoff
No .gitignore, because there's no repository. next-steps.md says to download discovery/[project]/ and aidlc/ from the Space into the build repository, never the data. aidlc/spaces/[space]/knowledge/… is AI-DLC's own folder layout and has nothing to do with AWS Quick Spaces: it is saved in the team Space like any other artifact.

Still to verify, per the design doc: how soon a second person's AWS Quick sees the driver's last write, and whether a Viewer can write at all. Until then, re-read before continuing after a recent save, and share the Space as Owners with anyone who might drive.

Practice

Recall first: the take-over prompt, from memory. Then open this.

"Continue the AI-DLC Discovery workshop for [project] from discovery/[project]/PROJECT.md in the [space] space. I'm taking over as [role]. Start at [step]."

It's the lost-chat recovery prompt from lesson 0008, plus two things: the Space, and who is taking over.

1. What changes in the method when a workshop runs in a team Space?
The phases, checkpoints, files, and paths stay the same. The Space only changes where they live, and adds one rule: one driver at a time.
2. Why is there only one driver at a time?
Two writers would overwrite each other's work. Many people can attach the Space and read at once; only the writing is single-file.
3. What does the skill do before every write to the Space?
If the driver is someone else, it stops and asks before writing. It also checks the text for personal data. AWS Quick may ask the driver to approve each save, but that's the tool, not the other Owners.
4. How do pods work in a team Space?
discovery/[project]-[pod]/ in the same Space, created after scoring, with the shared Signals and scoring artifacts copied in. Pods never write to each other's files; the facilitator sees them all.
5. At Handoff, what is aidlc/spaces/[space]/knowledge/?
The two "spaces" share a word and nothing else. AI-DLC's team knowledge is a folder path; in Space mode it is saved in the team Space like any other file, then downloaded into the build repository.
6. Someone is shared on the Space as a Viewer. What can they do?
Viewers follow; Owners can drive and take over. If a Viewer needs to drive, an Owner shares the Space with them as Owner.
7. From lesson 0002: Signals finds no data, in the Space or the folder. What does it offer?
It never writes data on the day, Space or not. Data written after the team's hypotheses can only echo them.
