Lesson 0007 · About 12 minutes

Handoff and the follow-ups

One document for someone who wasn't in the room, and the dates that turn a workshop into a decision that holds.

Your winJudge a Discovery brief the way the build will: know what each section holds and what never belongs in it, explain where the brief goes next (own team, agents, or AI-DLC), and leave the room with owners and dates for the 7-, 30-, and 60-day follow-ups.

What this phase is for

Handoff writes the Discovery brief: the single document that carries the decision, its evidence, and what is still unproven into whatever build follows. It's written for every decision, including pivot and park, so the evidence is never lost.discovery-handoff/SKILL.md, Overview. It needs every artifact from the day plus validate/decision.md, and runs 15:45 to 16:15.

Four steps

  1. Check the required artifacts. Thirteen files, from team-expectations.md to decision.md, plus a current-product map when there's a codebase. Anything missing is listed with the skill that produces it. It never writes a partial brief.
  2. Write the brief: exactly 15 sections, in order, under 200,000 characters, summarising and linking rather than copying.
  3. AI-DLC team knowledge and data protection. When the route is AI-DLC, every persona is written in full to aidlc/spaces/[space]/knowledge/aidlc-product-agent/discovery-personas.md, so AI-DLC's product agent loads them. With a local folder, discovery/data/ goes into .gitignore. In a team Space there is no repository, so no .gitignore: next steps say to download discovery/[project]/ and aidlc/ from the Space into the build repository, never the data. (aidlc/spaces/ is AI-DLC's own folder layout, not an AWS Quick Space.)
  4. Next steps. handoff/next-steps.md says what happens by decision, lists any conditions before the build, and the follow-ups, so what happens next doesn't live only in the chat.

What never goes in: architecture, technology choices, or code, and no customer account, CRM, or sales identifiers or revenue figures. Constraints can say a system must integrate ("works with the reservations system"); they never say how.

Section 14 is the honest part. The Air brief lists ten open questions, starting with the riskiest assumption: "Will a real disrupted passenger trust the v2 confirmation enough not to call?" Every Assumed and Needs evidence claim from the day ends up here, including the travel-agency promise from the prototype.Air discovery-brief.md, about 19,000 characters.

Where the brief goes

Own engineering or agents
The brief is the build's starting document.
AI-DLC (2.10.0+)
The brief replaces AI-DLC's own Ideation phase, and AI-DLC starts at Inception, reading the brief as its single input.awslabs/aidlc-workflows; README.md, "From the brief to a build". The skill prints the command and stops: the build is a separate session, often days later, in the repository where the team builds. /aidlc workshop for an AI-DLC workshop, /aidlc classic when the team continues on its own.
Pivot or park
What happens at the 30-day follow-up, from the decision file.

Air's next-steps.md opens with "Do this only after the conditions below are met": a commit that tests first holds the build command behind its three conditions.Air next-steps.md

Before the brief: confirm, change, or label as a proposal everything the assistant drafted on the team's behalf: the reason, interim targets, kill criteria, owners. The brief carries it to engineering as if the team had decided it.run-sheet.md, 15:45 Handoff.

Checkpoint: "Could an engineer who wasn't here start from this brief?" Ask the room; it's the one question the brief is written to pass.

Readout, then the follow-ups

Readout (16:15, 45 minutes). Each pod shows the sponsor its decision, the prototype on the Flow view, the success criteria, and the next steps, in under five minutes. Agree the next step and its owner before anyone leaves. When the sponsor asks "why not do what I believed?", answer with the evidence from Signals, not an opinion, then bring it back to the next step. A good readout move from Air: show the commercial director the "What the team expected" table, where price turned out to be 9% of feedback.run-sheet.md, 16:15 and "After the day".

WhenWhat has happened
7 daysThe business metric's baseline is in; the brief is in the build repository; the owners in next-steps.md have started.
By 30 daysReal users have tried the prototype (the personas are synthetic); open hypotheses have gone to real customers, with discovery-survey if useful; deferred use cases are re-scored with new evidence.
30 and 60 daysAdoption and the business metric against the success criteria and the kill criteria.

Then what the team learned feeds back into Signals for the next cycle. Discovery is a loop, not a one-off day.

Practice

Recall first: write as many of the 15 sections as you can, in order. Then open this.

1 Engagement reference · 2 Decision · 3 Problem and evidence · 4 Personas · 5 Prioritized use cases · 6 PR/FAQ summary · 7 Success criteria · 8 GTM · 9 Scope · 10 Prioritized backlog · 11 Prototype screens and flows · 12 Current product · 13 Constraints · 14 Open hypotheses · 15 Full artifacts.

A way to remember it: the decision first (1–2), why (3–5), what we'd announce and how we'd know (6–8), what to build (9–11), the ground it stands on (12–13), what's unproven (14), everything else (15).

1. At Handoff, the persona critique is missing. What does the skill do?
It never writes a partial brief. It lists what's missing and which skill produces it (here, discovery-personas in critique mode), and offers to run that step.
2. The engineering lead asks to add "use a serverless queue for rebooking events" to the brief. Your answer?
Discovery decides who, what, and why. Constraints can name a system that must integrate, but never how to build it. The feasibility unknown can go in section 14 as a question.
3. The team decides to park. Is a brief written?
A parked idea's evidence is worth keeping. next-steps.md says what would reopen it and what happens at 30 days.
4. The route is AI-DLC. What does the Handoff skill do about AI-DLC?
It prints the command and never starts AI-DLC: the build is a separate session in the build repository, often days later. AWS Quick doesn't run AI-DLC, and the brief replaces Ideation rather than feeding it.
5. Where does Handoff write the personas so AI-DLC's product agent loads them?
AI-DLC team knowledge, in the active space or "default". Handoff writes nothing else under aidlc/. discovery/data/ is raw data and never committed.
6. What goes into section 14 of the brief?
Every hypothesis Validate didn't settle, every claim still Assumed or Needs evidence, the riskiest assumption if open, and the top pre-mortem risks with warning signs, all as questions. The brief summarises and links; it doesn't copy.
7. What should have happened by the 30-day follow-up?
The personas are synthetic, so real users test the prototype and the open hypotheses by day 30. Adoption against the kill criteria comes at 30 and 60 days after launch.
8. Mixed review: which of these is not on Handoff's required-artifacts list?
The feature description is a step inside Working Backwards; its choice flows into the PR/FAQ and PRD, which are required. The team's expectations are required, so the brief can show what the team believed before the data.
