# AI-DLC Discovery Resources

## Knowledge

### In this repository (primary, highest trust)
- [README.md](../README.md)
  The five phases, the eleven skills, the artifact tree. Use for: the one-paragraph pitch and the phase table.
- [skills/aidlc-discovery/SKILL.md](../skills/aidlc-discovery/SKILL.md)
  Phase order, checkpoints, shared rules, the progress board, the "to start, it needs" table. Use for: the spine of the method.
- [skills/discovery-*/SKILL.md](../skills/)
  One skill per job, each with steps, outputs, and lessons learned. Use for: the detail of any one stage.
- [facilitator/run-sheet.md](../facilitator/run-sheet.md)
  The day by the clock, with "Say", "Watch", "Good looks like", and "Facilitator practice" per phase. Use for: timing and room craft.
- [facilitator/train-the-trainer.md](../facilitator/train-the-trainer.md)
  The half-day session for new facilitators, role cards, the failure drill, the "ready to facilitate" bar. Use for: designing training.
- [facilitator/reference-runs/anycompany-air/](../facilitator/reference-runs/anycompany-air/)
  A complete run on the default sample. Use for: real example artifacts on slides.

### Outside sources the method builds on
- [Werner Vogels, "Working Backwards" (All Things Distributed, 2006)](https://www.allthingsdistributed.com/2006/11/working_backwards.html)
  Amazon's press-release-first process. Use for: why the PR/FAQ comes before the prototype.
- [Marty Cagan, "The Four Big Risks" (SVPG)](https://www.svpg.com/four-big-risks/)
  Value, usability, feasibility, business viability. Use for: the four risks in Validate.
- [Gary Klein, "Performing a Project Premortem" (Harvard Business Review, 2007)](https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem)
  Imagine the project failed, then explain why. Use for: the pre-mortem in Validate.
- [awslabs/aidlc-workflows](https://github.com/awslabs/aidlc-workflows)
  AI-DLC itself. Use for: what happens after Handoff, and where the brief plugs in.

### On how the lessons are built
- [Elizabeth L. Bjork & Robert A. Bjork, "Making Things Hard on Yourself, But in a Good Way" (2011)](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/04/EBjork_RBjork_2011.pdf)
  Desirable difficulties: retrieval, spacing, interleaving. Use for: explaining why the quizzes come before the answers, and why lesson 0008 is shuffled.
