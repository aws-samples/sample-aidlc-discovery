# Design: telemetry as a signal source

Status: built (2026-10-30): the Air telemetry sample, its answer key, `references/telemetry-analysis.md` in Signals, the scoring, prototype, and validate hooks, the Now, Next, Later roadmap in Handoff, and checks O04 to O07.

## Problem

Signals today reads what customers **say**: reviews, calls, surveys, account notes, plus a few monthly business tables. It does not read what customers and systems **do**. Product engineering teams already have that in their monitoring tools (CloudWatch, Datadog, OpenTelemetry exports in S3): logs, traces, metrics, and often real user monitoring (RUM). The most useful signals sit where the two disagree: a loud complaint the system cannot see, or a silent failure nobody complains about.

## Decisions (agreed 2026-10-30)

1. **An addition, not a new data input.** "Production plus telemetry": telemetry has no customer voice, and alone it pushes teams toward the loudest error. Signal Briefs can carry telemetry summaries too.
2. **Design for RUM and for backend-only.** RUM shows where people give up; backend traces show where the system failed. The sample has both, and Signals labels a journey rebuilt from backend spans as weaker evidence than RUM.
3. **Aggregated at the source.** Small daily tables per journey step, never raw logs or traces: they are too large for a chat and carry personal data. At most four files.
4. **Sequence:** this sample and its answer key, then Signals, then a job that writes the source queries, then live access (MCP) for continuous discovery.

## The sample: `samples/anycompany-air/discovery/data/telemetry/`

All 1 May to 15 October 2026, generated by `scripts/samples/anycompany-air.py` with the existing seed, so it stays reproducible. Field names follow OpenTelemetry where one exists (`service.name`, `http.route`, `span.name`).

| File | Rows (about) | What it holds |
|------|-------------:|---------------|
| `journey-map.csv` | 15 | Each journey step, its order, its source (`rum` or `trace`), and the RUM view or `http.route` it maps to. The key that turns technical names into the customer's journey |
| `rum-journeys-daily.csv` | 4,000 | Per day, journey (`disruption_rebooking`, `booking`), step, and channel (`app`, `web`): sessions reaching the step, and median seconds on it |
| `spans-daily.csv` | 1,000 | Per day, `service.name` and `span.name` or `http.route`: requests, errors, p50 and p95 duration. Includes the agents' reissue tool and the notification sender |
| `errors-top.csv` | 25 | Per service, the top error codes with an anonymized message, a count, and first and last seen. No user IDs, IPs, or booking references |

About 300 KB in total, fine for a local folder or a Space.

### Journeys

**Disruption rebooking** (RUM): `alert_opened`, `trip_viewed`, `alternatives_viewed`, then one of `suggestion_kept` (today's only self-service action), `contact_us_tapped`, or exit. **Booking** (RUM): `search`, `results`, `fare_selected`, `extras`, `payment`, `confirmed`.

### Designed truths

1. **The silent top problem.** About 72% of disrupted passengers open the alert in the app or on the web. Of those, about 60% reach `alternatives_viewed`, and about 68% of those tap **Contact us** within two minutes of seeing them. The routes behind these steps return **zero server errors**: the journey fails as designed, so error dashboards show nothing. `suggestion_kept` matches the 12.2% self-service rate, and the Contact us taps line up with the 418 rebooking calls per 1,000 disrupted passengers. Feedback says it ("I saw those in the app too, why couldn't I pick one?"); telemetry shows how often, and that it is not a bug. Say and do agree: **Known**.
2. **The trap: payment timeouts.** In August, `POST /payments` has p95 latency above 4 seconds and 1.8% errors on the busiest days, the loudest thing on any error dashboard. Yet booking conversion that month is the highest of the period (3.17%) and the payment step's drop-off barely moves. Real, small, and the engineering-led choice. Feedback barely mentions it (one review about card payments failing).
3. **Feasibility evidence: reissue errors.** The agents' reissue tool (`reissue-service`) fails 4% to 6% of reissues, mostly `FARE_RULE_MISMATCH`, with peaks in July and August. This matches the 13 feedback records about reissue errors and wrong fees, and is evidence for the engineering lead's spike: a self-service flow built on this service inherits the failures.
4. **Telemetry only: late alerts.** The notification sender's span shows a median of 9 minutes and a p95 of 41 minutes from cancellation to alert sent in July and August. Nobody complains about the delay itself, but it explains why the good alternatives are gone when people look ("the flights they could see fill up"). A silent problem, labelled **Known (telemetry only)**, which becomes a pre-mortem reason and a candidate requirement (message timing).
5. **Counter-evidence.** The booking journey is healthy outside the August payment days: conversion steady, no step with a growing drop-off.

### Traps

- **Reading the error dashboard as the priority list.** Payment errors are real and small; the top problem has no errors.
- **Taking the alert-to-Contact-us drop-off as proof of intent.** It shows people did not self-serve; only the feedback says they wanted to. That is why telemetry is an addition, not a replacement.
- **Backend-only reasoning.** Without RUM, the disruption journey can only be rebuilt from span sessions; the sample marks which steps exist only in RUM, so a team without RUM sees what it would miss.

## What changes in the skill (step 2, after this sample)

- **Signals** finds `telemetry/` next to the feedback, reads `journey-map.csv` first, and reports per journey step: volume (reach), drop-off to the next step, errors and slow responses (severity), and repeat attempts. A new **Say and do** table puts each theme's feedback next to its telemetry: both agree (Known), feedback only (the system does not see it), telemetry only (a silent problem). Steps rebuilt only from backend spans are labelled weaker than RUM.
- **Scoring** uses telemetry for reach and the outcome baseline, and the reissue errors as feasibility evidence, still for the engineering lead to confirm.
- **Prototype** names the funnel step the test moment is meant to change.
- **Validate** writes success criteria as telemetry measures where they exist (for example, the share of disrupted sessions reaching a rebooked confirmation without `contact_us_tapped`), so the 30, 60, and 90-day follow-ups read the same dashboard.
- **Handoff** adds a **Now / Next / Later** section for the product owner: the build-first use case and its gates; the deferred candidates with the evidence that would move each up and who gets it; the rest with why. Outcomes and conditions, not features and dates.

## Answer key and tests

`facilitator/anycompany-air.md` gets a telemetry section with the truths and traps above, and new expectations for the end-to-end checks: the say-and-do table exists, the top problem is labelled as having no errors, the payment trap is not ranked above disruption, the late alerts appear as a telemetry-only signal, and the reissue errors appear as feasibility evidence.

## Later steps

- **Step 3, the telemetry brief job:** writes the queries a team runs in its own environment to produce the four tables: CloudWatch Logs Insights and RUM, Datadog queries, and Athena over OpenTelemetry exports in S3, with identifiers removed at the source.
- **Step 4, live access:** the CloudWatch and Datadog MCP servers, for a weekly Quick Flow in continuous discovery, after checking MCP works in AWS Quick.
